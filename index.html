Start here

One hundred is seventeen questions. Not twenty-five.

The AMC 10 gives 6 points for a correct answer, 1.5 points for a question left blank, and nothing for a wrong one. Sixteen right with three left blank clears 100. So does seventeen right. Either way, eight questions on that paper are not your problem.

Most students sitting in the 60s and 70s are not missing theory. They are spending eleven minutes on question 22 and never reaching question 14. This program works on both halves: the theory that earns the seventeen, and the judgment about where the other fifty-eight minutes go.

The structure

Four tracks, running in parallel.

Every AMC 10 is built from the same four areas. So is this program. Your child works all four every single week, instead of finishing one in September and having forgotten it by November.

Track 01

Algebra

Factoring and symmetric expressions. Telescoping, systems, exponents. Inequalities and AM-GM. Sequences. Polynomials and Vieta. Mean, median and data.

The biggest single block on the paper, and the one where speed compounds fastest.

Track 02

Geometry

Angle chasing in circles. Similarity and congruency. Coordinate geometry. Area by decomposition, shoelace and ratios. Circle geometry. 3D solids.

Where students burn the most minutes per point, usually by not drawing the auxiliary line.

Track 03

Combinatorics

Smart casework. Probability. Restrictions and complementary counting. Logic problems. Stars and bars, inclusion-exclusion, recursion.

The area where a wrong setup in the first thirty seconds costs you the whole problem.

Track 04

Number Theory

Modular arithmetic. Factors and prime thinking. Divisibility. Digits and decimal representation. Diophantine equations and bounding. Divisors and factorials.

The smallest block, and the most learnable from scratch inside eight weeks.

Week 0 · sent from Aug 25 · due before Sep 12

Questions

When does it actually start?
Wednesday, September 9, at 5:00 p.m. New York time. That session is the review of the diagnostic practice test, which I send out beforehand for your child to take on their own, under exam conditions. The four weekend lectures start on September 12 and 13. Week 0 modules should be done before that first weekend.
How much work is it outside class?
You get out what you put in. Five hours a week of live class, and a minimum of two hours a week on your own if 100 is the actual target. Every lecture comes with a handout and a problem set, and the students who clear 100 are the ones who do them. A student who attends the live hours and skips the problem sets will not get there, and I would rather say that now than in October.
What's the difference between this and the Basics camp?
The summer Basics camp covered AMC 10 problems 1 to 8. This one is built for the whole paper and a score above 100, with four parallel tracks and eight weeks of problem-solving running right up to the competition.
My child didn't do the summer camp. Can they still join?
Yes. This cohort is a mix of students who did the summer camp and students who are new to Shef Scholars. Week 0 is four self-study foundation modules across all four tracks. They go out from August 25, and within 48 hours of enrolling if you join after that, so new students start level with returning ones.
What if my child can't attend every session live?
Every session is recorded and shared privately, and recordings stay available through December 31. But recordings are a backup, not a plan. A student who watches most of this instead of attending it will get much less out of it.
My child already scores above 110. Should they join?
Probably not. This program spends its first six weeks building theory that a 110 student already has. Email me and I will tell you honestly what I think would help more.
How do we actually register for the AMC 10?
Through your child's school, or through an authorized testing center if the school does not host it. Families cannot register with the MAA directly, and sites usually close two to three weeks before the competition. Start in September. Enrolling in this program does not register your child for the exam. If you are stuck, book a call and I will help you find a site.
Which sessions are recorded, and is my child on camera?
All sessions are recorded, and yes. This is taught as an interactive class, not a lecture broadcast, so students join with their camera on and will appear in the recordings. Recordings are shared only with enrolled families through a private link, are never posted publicly or used in marketing, and stay available through December 31. If your child is ill or something comes up at home, email me and we will work around it for that session.
Who actually teaches the sessions?
I, Zlatko-Salko Lagumdzija, teach at least 32 of the 40. For up to 8 of them I bring in a former olympiad competitor to lead the session. They are people who have solved these problems under real pressure, and a second voice working through a problem is worth more than a fortieth hour of mine.
What's the refund policy?
Full refund through September 14. By then your child will have taken the diagnostic practice test, sat the review session on September 9, and attended all four lectures on September 12 and 13. If it is not the right fit after that, email me by September 14 and I will refund you in full. Is this affiliated with the MAA or the AMC?
No. We are not affiliated with the Mathematical Association of America, and nothing here is endorsed by them. We prepare students to do their best on their competition.
Where do classes happen?
Live on Google Meet. You get the link and the calendar invites as soon as you enroll, along with the Week 0 modules.
Do you guarantee a score of 100?
No, and be careful of anyone who does. Every student starts somewhere different and progress depends on the work they put in. What is guaranteed is the structure, the material, and my attention, which is why the cohort is capped at 30.
